Abstract. This paper develops a cost-effective sequential route recommender system to 
provide real-time routing recommendations for vacant taxis searching for the next passen -
ger. We propose a prediction-and-optimization framework to recommend the searching 
route that maximizes the expected profit of the next successful passenger pickup based on 
the dynamic taxi demand-supply distribution. Specifically, this system features a deep 
learning-based predictor that dynamically predicts the passenger pickup probability on a 
road segment and a recursive searching algorithm that recommends the optimal searching 
route. The predictor integrates a graph convolution network (GCN) to capture the spatial 
distribution and a long short-term memory (LSTM) to capture the temporal dynamics of 
taxi demand and supply. The GCN-LSTM model can accurately predict the pickup proba -
bility on a road segment with the consideration of potential taxi oversupply. Then, the 
dynamic distribution of pickup probability is fed into the route optimization algorithm to 
recommend the optimal searching routes sequentially as route inquiries emerge in the sys -
tem. The recursion tree-based route optimization algorithm can significantly reduce the 
computational time and provide the optimal routes within seconds for real-time imple -
mentation. Finally, extensive experiments using Beijing Taxi GPS data demonstrate the 
effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed recommender system.

1. Introduction
The availability of global positioning system (GPS) trajectory data with rich information of human mobility pro -
vides an unprecedented opportunity for real-time mobile recommender systems, including location-based market -
ing (Hopkins and Turner 2012 ), public transit systems (Du et al. 2019 ), travel tour recommendation (Ge et al. 2014 ), 
and ride-hailing service (Feng et al. 2020 ). Despite many studies on human mobility pattern discovery and traffic 
demand prediction using GPS data, taxi companies are facing a business opportunity and a technical challenge of 
exploiting massive taxi GPS data to provide direct guidance for vacant taxis in search of their next passenger (Ge 
et al. 2010 ). Different from the mobility-on-demand ride-hailing systems (Alonso-Mora et al. 2017 , Braverman et al. 
2019 , Feng et al. 2020 , Zhu et al. 2021 ), such as DiDi, Uber, and Lyft, taxi companies in urban cities, such as Beijing 
Taxi and NYC Yellow Cab, provide street-hail services where taxi drivers search for the next passenger through a 
searching route according to their experience and habit. However, because of the lack of local information on pas -
senger distribution and surrounding taxi activities, taxi drivers are constantly concerned about the difficulties in 
finding the next passenger and the low profit due to competition among vacant taxis.
Recent efforts have been made to develop intelligent mobile route recommender systems for taxi drivers who 
offer street-hail services, aiming to optimize the searching route for vacant taxis (Xiao et al. 2020 ). Such a mobile 
route recommender system suggests a searching strategy for a taxi driver subscribed to this service, as soon as the

Garg and Ranu 2018 , Qu et al. 2020 ). The last group of methods, represented by Qu et al. ( 2014 ), focuses on optimiz -
ing the net profit of the searching route offered to a single taxi by combining the passenger pickup probability and 
expected profit.
Despite the previous efforts, developing a practical cost-effective route recommender system that responds in 
real-time remains challenging. First, to provide route recommendations, an accurate prediction of passenger pickup 
probability is desired at the road segment level. Existing studies of traffic demand prediction, including the autore -
gressive methods (Li et al. 2012 , Moreira-Matias et al. 2013 ), recurrent neural networks (Xu et al. 2017 ), and graph 
convolution network (GCN)-based predictors (Jiang and Luo 2021 ), were developed based on the temporal or spa -
tial dependencies of traffic speed or traffic flow. However, the taxi route recommendation requires a dynamic pas -
senger pickup predictor, which remains technically challenging because of multiple impact factors, such as time, 
road network, weather conditions, traffic conditions, and simultaneous modeling of taxi supply and demand. Sec -
ond, the pickup probability predictor needs to be embedded into a route optimization strategy to maximize the 
expected profit. Because of the large route searching space in a complex road network and a large number of taxis 
requesting recommendation solutions, a real-time sequential route searching algorithm remains unexplored. It is 
also vital to avoid recommendation overload, which is a phenomenon due to the repeated recommendations of the 
same searching route to too many taxi drivers, causing taxi congestion, and profit decline (Qu et al. 2014 , Xiao et al. 
2020 ). However, existing taxi recommender systems, including the aforementioned energy-efficient, shortest-route, 
and cost-effective recommender systems, are supported by offline recommendation algorithms that do not care -
fully address the issue of recommendation overload.
To address the aforementioned technical challenges, we propose a new cost-effective sequential route recom -
mendation method, combining a deep learning-based prediction model for road segment-level pickup probabil -
ity predictions and a recursive route searching algorithm for real-time optimal route recommendations. 
Specifically, the deep learning-based prediction model is based on a GCN and long short-term memory (LSTM) 
to capture the dynamic human mobility patterns and taxi demand-supply distribution from both topological 
and temporal perspectives. Meanwhile, context information, such as weather conditions, is also considered to 
improve prediction accuracy. Different from the existing GCN-based traffic demand or speed prediction meth -
ods (Jiang and Luo 2021 ), the proposed GCN-LSTM model is developed for passenger pickup probability pre -
diction, which integrates the long-term and short-term patterns of taxi demand-supply distribution in a 
topological road network. As such, it captures the neighborhood taxi searching behavior and customer demand 
changes caused by previous recommendations, enabling a more timely and precise prediction of pickup proba -
bility that dynamically balances the taxi demand and supply to avoid recommendation overload. Furthermore, 
to improve computational efficiency for route optimization, we integrate the pickup probability predictor with 
a recursive route searching algorithm to support real-time route recommendations for a sequence of taxi drivers. 
The pickup probabilities are updated dynamically according to surrounding taxis’ previously scheduled driv -
ing routes to avoid recommendation overload. We take the distribution of pickup probability as an input to esti -
mate the potential profit of candidate searching routes represented by a recursion tree structure, significantly 
reducing the size of route searching space for real-time recommendations. As such, we can serve more than 
3,200 taxis in a large road network consisting of 18,301 road segments, as demonstrated in our numerical experi -
ments using the Beijing Taxi GPS data. The performance of the proposed system sets a new benchmark against 
the state-of-the-art route recommender systems for taxi drivers and confirms its effectiveness in addressing the 
recommendation overload issue.

present the proposed sequential route recommender system framework. Sections 4 and 5 introduce in detail the 
proposed dynamic pickup probability prediction model and sequential route optimization model, respectively. 
Numerical experiments using real-world data from Beijing Taxi are reported in Section 6 . Finally, Section 7 con -

2.1. Traffic Demand Prediction
The prediction of traffic demand has been investigated as a key to the success of intelligent transportation systems 
(Yuan and Li 2021 ). Early studies of traffic demand were focused on understanding the spatiotemporal patterns of 
taxi trips and taxi driving behavior (Hochmair 2016 , Xu et al. 2016 ). Detecting hot spots of taxi traffic has been stud -
ied for taxi relocation, aiming to find the regions with high taxi demand (Moreira-Matias et al. 2013 , Xu et al. 2017 , 
Yao et al. 2018 ) or extreme traffic conditions (Li et al. 2021 ). In terms of forecasting methodologies, one line of the lit -
erature considers traffic demand prediction as a time series forecasting problem and builds on autoregressive inte -
grated moving average (ARIMA)-based autoregressive models to predict passenger demands in certain hot spots 
and taxi stands (Li et al. 2012 , Moreira-Matias et al. 2013 ). Chang et al. ( 2010 ) improved taxi demand prediction 
accuracy by integrating context information of the time, weather, and taxi location. The other line of related work 
uses machine learning and deep sequence models to improve prediction accuracy. Xu et al. ( 2017 ) and Zhang et al. 
( 2022 ) implemented recurrent neural networks for taxi demand hot spots detection. Yu et al. ( 2017 ) built a deep neu -
ral network based on LSTM for traffic prediction in extreme conditions. A deep multiview spatial-temporal net -
work model by integrating the LSTM model and the convolutional neural network (CNN) was developed for taxi 
pickup demand in big regions (0.7 km × 0.7 km; Yao et al. 2018 ).
More recently, the success of graph neural networks (GNNs) has shown superior performance on modeling graph- 
based networks (Wu et al. 2020a ), such as document citation networks (Thekumparampil et al. 2018 , Liu et al. 2020 ), 
social networks (Fan et al. 2019 , Ma et al. 2021 ), and network-based traffic demand prediction (Jiang and Luo 2021 ). 
With the great potential in road network modeling, the gated attention networks (GaANs) (Zhang et al. 2018 ) and the 
multivariate graph neural network (MTGNN) (Wu et al. 2020b ) demonstrated the advances of graph modeling in traf -
fic flow prediction. A newly emerging GNN-based model is the GCN, which enables the modeling of spatial distribu -
tion as topological graphs (Kipf and Welling 2017 ). The GCN has excellent advantages for spatial traffic demand 
analysis in a road network once combined with the temporal dynamic property. Lin et al. ( 2018 ) implemented GCN 
to learn hidden heterogeneous pairwise correlations between bike stations to predict station-level traffic demand in a 
bike-sharing network. Other GCN variants were proposed to study traffic flow with a multigraph convolutional neu -
ral network model (Chai et al. 2018 ) and an attention-based spatial-temporal graph convolutional network (Guo et al. 
2019 ). Du et al. ( 2020 ) proposed a dynamic transition GCN to simultaneously extract temporal and spatial features to 
obtain accurate demand predictions for taxi transition demand between origin-destination (OD) pairs.
Indeed, existing regional traffic speed and traffic demand prediction methods have built the foundation for intel -
ligent mobile recommendation systems that suggest the optimal routes between OD pairs with the minimum driv -
ing time or the hot spots with large taxi demand. However, because of the following three limitations, they are not 
directly applicable to the cost-effective route recommendation that maximizes taxi profit. First, the current autore -
gressive methods, recurrent neural networks, and GCN-based methods were developed based on the temporal or 
spatial dependencies of one single factor of either traffic speed or traffic flow. However, maximizing the expected 
profit of a searching route depends on an accurate pickup probability estimation, which requires simultaneous 
modeling of taxi supply and demand distribution. The supply distribution depicts taxis’ searching behaviors and 
the demand distribution profiles the locations of future customers. Second, existing GCN-based methods were 
developed to model the short-term dependencies of traffic demand. As both taxis’ searching behavior and custo -
mers’ demand distribution have daily and hourly patterns, it is desirable to capture the long-term dependencies in 
the road network in addition to the short-term dependencies. Finally, existing mobile recommender systems have 
not considered how recommendations made to current taxis would change future pickup probability. To wit, Qu 
et al. ( 2014 ) first pointed out the importance of taxi pickup probability on road segments for the cost-effective route 
recommender systems; Qu et al. ( 2014 ) and Sun et al. ( 2017 ) only considered a simple historical mean method to 
estimate pickup probability; and Yuan et al. ( 2011 ) considered the spatial dependency of pickup probability in road 
clusters. To close the aforementioned research gaps and improve the accuracy of pickup probability prediction, this 
paper extends the GCN-based methods by integrating the long-term and short-term patterns of taxi demand- 
supply distributions in a topological road network, as well as by considering the mutual dependence of current 
recommendations and future pickup probability distributions.
2.2. Mobile Route Recommender System
The route recommender systems for taxi drivers aim to leverage historical taxi GPS traces for improving recom -
mendation performance. It has been formulated with various objectives to provide routing suggestions for a vacant 
taxi searching for the next passenger. Ge et al. ( 2010 ) developed an energy-efficient mobile recommender system

for an optimal sequence of waiting points for the next passenger. Yuan et al. ( 2010 , 2011 , 2013a , b ) and Ge et al. 
( 2021 ) worked on a series of intelligent taxi services, such as pickup point detection and driving direction recom -
mendation. Kong et al. ( 2017 ) further developed a recommender system with a time-location combined service rec -
ommendation to provide top N recommended passenger searching areas. However, these studies only focus on 
pickup point recommendations while ignoring the potential profit of exact searching routes. Qu et al. ( 2014 ) first 
provided an entire driving route instead of discrete pickup points based on the expected profit for taxi drivers. Sim -
ilar research has been conducted to recommend searching routes for taxi drivers with the objective of minimizing 
the waiting time or the searching distance (Wu et al. 2017 , Garg and Ranu 2018 , Qu et al. 2020 , Ye et al. 2019 ). Ye et al. 
( 2018 ) compared the recommender systems with different objective functions, and Wang et al. ( 2019 ) found that 
taxi drivers could improve hourly earnings by selecting long trips rather than aiming for cruising-time reduction. 
However, both the relocation and route recommendation did not consider the recommendation overload issue in a 
real-time environment for a sequence of vacant taxi drivers searching in the same region.
2.3. Recommendation Overload
Traditional recommender systems may repeatedly recommend the same or similar items to a group of customers, 
resulting in system-generated best-selling products and a low recommendation diversity that decreases users’ pur -
chase intention and recommendation effectiveness. Similarly, recommendation overload can happen in a route rec -
ommender system if the same route is recommended to too many drivers in the same area (Qu et al. 2014 ). Qu et al. 
( 2014 ) introduced a top K sequential recommendation framework that compares trajectory similarities between 
subsequently recommended routes to mitigate the influence of prior recommendations made to surrounding taxis. 
The top K strategy avoids recommending the same route repeatedly, but it does not consider the profit difference 
on alternative routes and may direct taxis to a route with a low expected profit. Xiao et al. ( 2020 ) proposed a multi -
user mobile sequential recommendation system that recommends nonoverlapped searching routes to a group of 
taxis with a road capacity constraint. However, this system assumes that taxis start searching simultaneously and is 
not applicable for sequential recommendations when taxis come in a sequence. In real-world practices, a recom -
mendation should be made within seconds of a submitted query. Thus, the demand and supply distribution can 
change as recommended routes are sent to taxi drivers. However, the existing route recommender systems do not 
incorporate recommended searching routes for future route recommendations. In this paper, we will achieve a bal -
anced taxi supply and demand distribution by considering the potential recommendation overload.

Definition 5 ( Virtual Station ). Taxi, as a station-free transportation mode, can have arbitrary passenger pickup 
locations scattered around urban areas. During certain periods, traffic demands tend to congregate in certain

during other time periods.

4. Dynamic Passenger Pickup Probability Prediction
The pickup probability of each road segment captures how likely a taxi driving through the road segment would 
find a passenger. It can be affected by the spatial-temporal dynamics of taxi activities and human mobility. Specifi -
cally, in metropolitan areas such as Beijing, it is frequently observed that demands for taxi services concentrate on 
hot-spot areas and decrease progressively moving away from these spots. Meanwhile, passengers’ daily mobility 
clearly shows different patterns, varying from peak to off-peak hours. A precise prediction of pickup probability 
requires the model to learn taxi activities and human mobility from dynamic graph-structured road network data, 
which is a challenging task.
With the recent development of the GCN, some static graph-based networks, such as document citation net -
works, social networks, and knowledge graphs, have been modeled with promising results (Kipf and Welling 
2017 ). GCN learns hidden layer representations that encode both the local graph structure and the features of neigh -
borhood nodes and has been proven to scale linearly in the number of graph edges. To develop a dynamic sequen -
tial mobile route recommender system, we propose a GCN to model the spatial distribution of taxi demand-supply 
in a road network as a topological graph. The GCN model, with its topology modeling features, has great potential 
in modeling the spatial structure required to predict the taxi pickup probability. A prominent one is its ability to 
model the diffusion of the node states to the neighborhood, which can be considered an abstraction of the demand 
distribution for taxi services in the road network. On the other hand, a recurrent neural network architecture called 
LSTM (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997 ) is suitable for modeling the temporal pattern of the pickup probability. 
LSTM has demonstrated its power in many sequential pattern learning and time series forecasting tasks, such as 
speech recognition, language translation, and traffic forecasting. LSTM can capture the long-term dependencies 
between time-series observations and the short-term ones, which resemble the daily and hourly demand patterns 
for taxi services. Therefore, we propose an integrated framework based on GCN and LSTM, in which the GCN cap -
tures the spatial dependency of the taxi demand-supply distribution in a road network, and the LSTM captures the 
temporal dynamics in pickup probability time series.

5. Sequential Route Recommendation
With the GCN-LSTM pickup probability predictor, the sequential route recommender system can be developed to 
sequentially optimize the searching route for vacant taxis in search of their next passenger. The sequential recom -
mendation follows the FIFO rule, and the searching route is recommended to the taxi driver as soon as the query 
emerges. In this section, we first propose a recursive route searching strategy for a single taxi and then present the 
sequential route recommendation for a sequence of vacant taxis.

5.2. Sequential Route Recommendation
The recursive route searching algorithm can provide an optimal searching route for a single taxi. However, in 
real-world practices, taxis may arrive in a sequence, and the road condition, such as taxi supply, will change 
dynamically. For a newly emerged query, the route recommender system should consider previously scheduled 
taxis currently searching around to avoid recommendation overload. Hence, we provide a sequential RRS (SRRS) 
by integrating the dynamic pickup probability predictor and the recursive route searching algorithm for a 
sequence of vacant taxis.

6. Numerical Experiments and Discussions
In this section, we demonstrate the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed sequential route recommender sys -
tem with extensive experiments using the Beijing Taxi data. All experiments were conducted on a server with 2X 
10-core Intel Xeon Gold 5215 Processor, 1 TB RAM, and 10X 2080Ti GPUs. The Python scripts for all numerical 
experiments presented in this section can be found at the GitHub site of this paper (Liu et al. 2023 ).
6.1. Road Segment Pickup Probability Prediction
6.1.1. Baselines and Evaluation Metrics. The optimal hyperparameters of the proposed GCN-LSTM model are 
found through random searching (Bergstra and Bengio 2012 ). Specifically, we use one layer of graph convolution 
with dimension 32 and one layer of LSTM with dimension 16. The learning rate is 0.01, and the batch size is 8. To 
validate the effectiveness of the pickup probability predictor, the following baseline methods are selected for per -
formance comparison: 
1. Classic Regressors. The random forest (RF) (Breiman 2001 ) and gradient boosting trees (GBT) (Friedman 
2002 ), two ensemble models for machine learning tasks, are used as classic baselines. They have been proven to be 
robust and effective but cannot deal with temporal and topological data by design (Maclin and Opitz 1997 ).
2. Autoregressive Models. The ARIMA and ARIMA with exogenous (ARIMA-X), two autoregressive models 
(Box et al. 2015 ), are used as linear autoregressive baselines. These two methods mainly focus on discovering tem -
poral patterns from historical data.
3. Nonlinear Autoregressors. The nonlinear autoregressive network with exogenous inputs (NARX) and LSTM 
(Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997 ) have the ability to process the sequential data by modeling a nonlinear rela -
tionship between the time series values and exogenous variables.
4. GNN-based Methods. The MTGNN (Wu et al. 2020b ) and GaAN (Zhang et al. 2018 ), two methods designed 
for traffic speed prediction, have the ability to learn spatiotemporal dynamics of road networks.
5. Benchmark Models. The historical average method from Qu et al. ( 2014 ) and the deep LSTM from Yu et al. 
( 2017 ) are two latest benchmark models for the taxi recommendation in the literature.
The prediction accuracy is measured on an hourly basis using the mean absolute error (MAE) metric, which mea -
sures the absolute error of pickup probability prediction. Furthermore, we calculate the weighted average of the 
MAE (wMAE), weighted by the total number of trips during each time slot (denoted as W(t)):

time series also impact the graph convolution performance of MTGNN because it does not explicitly model the 
short-term and long-term impact as our framework.
• Compared with the GaAN model, the proposed GCN-LSTM model benefits from the choice of the LSTM unit, 
which provides better temporal dynamics modeling of a sequence of graph convolution networks.

measured by the training time and execution time of the models. Specifically, the training time includes the compu -
tational time for formatting training data and model training until convergence, and the execution time includes 
the computational time for formatting input data and making predictions. As some baseline algorithms do not sup -

comparison is reported in the last column of Table 3 . It is seen that the proposed GCN-LSTM model can be trained

a high frequency of every five minutes. A well-trained GCN-LSTM model can update the distribution of pickup 
probability in the searching area in 13 sets, which is applicable for the online route recommendation. It takes a 
much longer time for the MTGNN model to converge, whereas the GaAN and GCN-LSTM models are more effi -
cient using the traffic network data. Compared with the NARX and LSTM methods that treat road segments inde -
pendently, the GCN-LSTM method is more efficient by batch learning the model parameters in the network.

update frequency of once per query.

Figure 9(b) is recommended. Overall, the sequential route recommendation with dynamic pickup probability 
update can help address the recommendation overload issue when there is an oversupply.

6.3.3. Efficiency Test. Finally, to evaluate the computing efficiency of the proposed C-SRR system, we use the case 
described in Section 6.2 when there is a taxi oversupply and the case of a three-day system implementation for real- 
world queries. Because the depth of the recursion tree affects the computing efficiency the most, we measure the 
query response time by varying the tree depth from 5 to 12 (roughly equivalent to 5 to 18 minutes of searching time 
limit τ). The result of the query response times is presented in Figure 8(b) . The bottom line shows the query 
response time for a queue of taxis starting searching on the same road network, with the bar on each data point 
representing the standard deviation. The computational time for the recursion tree construction is excluded and the 
response time indicates the efficiency of route optimization. It can be seen that the recursive searching algorithm is 
efficient and the response time is less than 1 second if the searching depth is no more than nine segments. However, 
the computational time increases exponentially with search depth and the response time can go up to 10 seconds 
when the search depth reaches 12. The upper line in Figure 8(b) presents the response time of a three-day imple -
mentation. The response time increases as the search depth increases, but the query response for a search depth of 8 
is less than 1.1 seconds, which is acceptable for a real-time online recommender system.
6.4. Active Region of Virtual Stations
Sometimes, waiting in a virtual station can be more profitable than searching along a route, especially when the cur -
rent searching-start segment of a taxi is near such a virtual station. On the one hand, if the current location is far 
away from any virtual stations, the taxi will waste gas and time driving to a virtual station, resulting in a low net 
profit per minute. In those cases, the waiting strategy is inferior to the route searching strategy. On the other hand, 
during certain time periods and when the searching-start segments are within certain regions, the waiting strategy 
could be superior to the route searching strategy. The set of searching-start segments R

recommended with a virtual station, we label this searching-start road segment as an active waiting segment. The

ture taxi demand-supply distribution as a topological structure in a road network, and the LSTM model is designed 
to model the temporal dynamics of the road network. Based on a timely and precise prediction of pickup probabili -
ties, the recommender system can achieve the balance of demand-and-supply mobility and avoid recommendation 
overload. To improve the computational efficiency of route optimization, we further propose a recursive route search -
ing algorithm to support the real-time recommender system for a sequence of taxi drivers. The algorithm takes the

the size of route searching space. In addition, the algorithm parameters are updated dynamically according to the pre -
viously scheduled driving routes to avoid recommendation overload. The route searching strategy is then compared 
with the waiting strategy by discovering profitable virtual stations, incentivized by experienced taxi drivers who pre -
fer waiting for passengers at hot spots. Our data have shown that such virtual stations change over time during the 
day. Finally, we conduct extensive experiments on real-world data sets collected from Beijing and the experimental 
results clearly validate the effectiveness of the proposed recommender system.
This paper can be extended in several future directions. The proposed recommender system considers the 
expected earnings of the next pickup for a single taxi driver searching in a localized region. The localized sequential 
search follows the first-come-first-serve policy that responds to route queries reactively. It would be beneficial to 
design a multistep long-term taxi demand predictor to proactively guide taxis to the regions with high taxi demand 
if the nearby road network has a very low pickup probability during off-peak hours. In addition, the batch recom -
mendation can be an alternative strategy to the sequential search during peak hours. The batch recommendation 
seeks a global earning for a group of taxis but requires drivers to wait for a batch of route queries from this group.

other mobile recommender systems. Although the proposed system is developed to provide street-hail services, 
the combination of a GCN-LSTM predictor and recursive route recommendation algorithm can help improve the

crowdsourced delivery services (Alnaggar et al. 2021 ) by recommending driving and delivering routes for new 
orders while avoiding the competition of delivery agents in the same region.
